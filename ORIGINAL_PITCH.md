## 1. The Master Thesis

The entire business rests on one idea: **trust is the scarce thing in the secondary AI hardware market, and the flow of that hardware exists whether AI booms or busts.**

AI accelerators now refresh every 18 to 36 months, down from the old 5 to 7 year server cycle. The first wave of GPU servers from the 2022 to 2024 boom is retiring between 2026 and 2029. That creates a permanent river of used hardware. On the other side, new parts take 26 weeks to two years to ship, and a dead GPU in a live cluster is downtime measured in dollars per hour. Flood of supply on one side, starving urgent demand on the other.

The only people standing in the middle today are gray-market resellers nobody trusts, because a buyer cannot tell if a used GPU actually works, how degraded it is, or whether the last owner's model weights and training data are still sitting on the drive. We become the trusted middle. Tested working, data wiped, guaranteed. We start as a broker who holds nothing and grow into the dealer who owns the price index for the whole market.

**Why it survives both outcomes.** If AI keeps booming, refresh churn never stops and we ride steady volume. If AI crashes, everyone dumps hardware at once and prices collapse, which is the single best buying environment a dealer can ask for. We are not betting AI goes up. We are betting it keeps moving, and it always moves.

**The timing correction.** The crash is the payday, not the start date. Distressed buyers who clean up in a downturn are the ones who already had the channels, the cash, and the process in place before it hit. If you launch from zero the day prices crater, you lose the window to setup work while prepared players take the deals. So: build the rails now, in the boom, while demand is healthy and you can learn the trade at a profit. Arrange your buying power in advance as a credit line or a small committed fund. Then when the market turns, you deploy fast and buy cheap while everyone else panics. Boom funds the education. Bust funds the empire.

## 2. The Business

A verified marketplace and dealer for retired data-center GPUs and high-value server components.

What we actually sell is certainty. A used H100 is worth whatever you can prove about it. We provide three proofs on every unit: it works (tested and graded), the data is gone (certified, auditable wipe), and it is backed (warranty). That stamp is the product. The metal is just the thing the stamp rides on.

We focus on chips, not chassis. GPUs are the only parts with enough value per square foot to justify a warehouse and the theft risk. A pallet of accelerators is worth millions. A pallet of cables is worth scrap.

The model in three words: verify, hold or broker, trade fast.

## 3. Who We Buy From and Who We Sell To

**Supply, who we pitch to buy from**

- Failed and pivoting AI startups liquidating clusters. Distressed, fast, cheap, and plentiful right now. Best supply. Watch bankruptcy filings and asset auctions.
- Enterprises upgrading H100 and A100 fleets to current-generation silicon.
- Smaller GPU clouds rotating out older gear.
- ITAD firms and e-recyclers with overflow they would rather wholesale than remarket. Be their outlet.
- Colocation operators clearing abandoned tenant hardware.

Pitch to sellers: I pay fast and I take the data-security liability off your hands with a certified wipe and a certificate of destruction. You get cash and a clean compliance trail instead of a depreciating pallet you have to babysit and a legal risk you do not want.

**Demand, who we sell to**

- AI startups locked out of new allocation or short on cash. They take used H100s to stretch runway.
- University and research labs. Chronically broke, locked out of new supply, ideal repeat buyers.
- Second-tier GPU clouds building capacity on a budget.
- Mid-market companies standing up internal AI without hyperscaler money.
- Repair shops and integrators who need a replacement chip now for a dead node. The downtime buyer pays the highest premium.
- Inference operators happy to run last-generation silicon at a discount.

Pitch to buyers: verified working, data-clean, warrantied data-center GPUs at a fraction of new, shipping this week instead of in 26 weeks. The trustworthy version of the gray market.

## 4. Roadmap

**Phase 1, months 0 to 12. Broker on consignment. Prove it with a phone and a test bench.**
Hold nothing you have not pre-sold or taken on consignment. Match sellers to buyers, take a spread or commission. Take gear into your space on consignment, test it, wipe it, sell it, then pay the seller their cut. You move real hardware without fronting inventory cash. Lock one verification process and one certified wipe standard. Win condition: a stack of closed deals, a known take rate, repeat buyers and sellers who call you first, and a private dataset of what every part actually trades for. The dataset is the real asset.

**Phase 2, months 12 to 30. Verified marketplace and a small warehouse.**
Productize the brokering: listings, verified badges, escrow, wipe certificates, live pricing. Lease a small secure warehouse. Begin holding inventory only on the highest-velocity parts you have proven move fast. Hire a hardware tech and an ops person. Two-sided network effects start to bite.

**Phase 3, years 3 to 5 and out. Dealer, price index, and crash war chest.**
Buy whole retired clusters cheap, tear them down, stock what moves, sell into urgency at margin. Turn your trade data into the price index everyone benchmarks against, the Kelley Blue Book for AI compute. Add a repair and warranty arm. Have committed buying power ready so that when the market turns, you buy the dip at scale.

## 5. Capital. How Much to Raise and What It Buys

Raise in tranches tied to milestones. Do not raise for inventory before you know what turns.

**Pre-seed, Phase 1: 25,000 to 75,000 dollars.**
Mostly self-funded or friends and family. Covers: LLC setup, export-control and data-privacy legal setup (the most important early spend), a GPU test rig, certified wipe tooling and process, basic insurance, a simple site, and first outreach. You can run this lean off existing income. This is the only phase a college student starts cold, and it is the on-ramp to everything else.

**Seed, Phase 2: 500,000 to 1,500,000 dollars.**
Raise only after Phase 1 proves a take rate and repeat demand. Covers: small warehouse lease, two to four staff, test lab build-out, marketplace software, working capital to start owning the fastest-moving inventory, a real compliance program, and theft and liability insurance. Milestone to unlock: proven margin, repeat customers, a meaningful number of closed deals, and the pricing dataset.

**Growth and crash war chest, Phase 3: 3,000,000 to 15,000,000 dollars, split by type.**
Structure this as two different kinds of money. Raise **equity** for the platform, team, and brand, the parts that compound. Use **asset-backed debt**, a credit facility secured against inventory, for the hardware itself. This is the sophisticated move: you do not give away ownership to buy GPUs you will flip in 60 days, you borrow against them. The credit line is also your dry powder for the crash. You arrange it in the boom so you can deploy it the moment prices crater.

**The rule:** equity buys the things that last, debt buys the things that turn. Never burn equity on melting inventory.

## 6. Unit Economics, the Honest Version

You make money on velocity, not on holding. A held GPU is a melting ice cube that can lose 40 percent of its value in 60 days.

- Broker phase: take a commission or spread per deal, zero carrying cost, near-pure margin on your time.
- Dealer phase: buy below market, sell into urgency above your cost, but every day in the warehouse eats your margin. Price to move, not to squeeze. Turns per quarter matter more than margin per unit.
- The warranty reserve: used hardware fails, so set aside a slice of every sale to cover returns. Underprice this and the returns eat you.

The compounding asset is not any single trade. It is the proprietary price data, the trusted brand, and the network. Those are what a buyer eventually pays a premium for and a competitor cannot copy.

## 7. Pre-Mortem. Where This Goes Wrong, and the Fix

1. **Inventory value decay.** Fix: stay asset-light until proven, hold only fast movers, finance inventory with debt not equity, price for speed.
2. **Export controls.** Advanced GPUs sit under US export law and the rules shift. Sell to the wrong buyer or country and it is a federal case, not a bad trade. Fix: screen every buyer and destination, document everything, get real export counsel from day one. The gray market ignores this and it will eventually bury them. This is where your AI law interest becomes a weapon, not a hobby.
3. **Data-security liability.** You are absorbing the seller's risk that model weights or training data remain on the drives. Fix: certified, auditable erase on every part with memory, every time, to a recognized standard. Done right it is your moat. Done sloppy it ends you.
4. **Demand softening in a crash.** If AI craters, some buyers vanish too. Fix: know the bargain-buyer segments that grow in a downturn, inference shops, researchers, and second-tier clouds buying cheap capacity. Supply floods, but those buyers feast too.
5. **Fraud and counterparty risk.** Fake, dead, or stolen GPUs, and payment fraud. Fix: your verification process plus escrow is the answer. It is also your whole reason to exist.
6. **Theft.** High value-density inventory is a target. Fix: real security and insurance on the building.

## 8. The Moat Ladder

- Early: hustle, speed, and trust. You out-care the gray market.
- Middle: two-sided network effects plus your proprietary pricing data.
- Late: inventory velocity plus the price index plus your name being the one people trust.

Each rung is harder to climb than the last, which is exactly what protects you once you are up there.

## 10. First 30 and 90 Days

**Next 30 days, costs almost nothing.**

- Incorporate and talk to an export-control and data-privacy attorney before anything else.
- Build the verification loop on paper: receive, test, grade, wipe, certify, list, sell, pay seller. Define the exact wipe standard.
- Study Setna iO directly, the teardown-and-trade aftermarket playbook is theirs. They are a short drive from you.
- Line up one seller with a small lot on consignment and one buyer, and run a single chip through your full loop. One closed cycle hands you your real margin, your real turn time, and your real process, with zero inventory cash at risk.

**Next 90 days.**

- Repeat the loop until you have a known take rate and repeat customers on both sides.
- Start logging every trade into your private price dataset. This becomes the index later.
- Only then write the seed deck, using real closed-deal numbers instead of projections. One brokered deal in hand is worth more than any forecast on a slide.

The whole plan compresses to this: prove the loop by hand, build the trust layer and the data in the boom, arrange your buying power in advance, and be the prepared buyer holding cash and channels when the crash hands you the inventory of a lifetime.
